.. _preamble-0027:

.. admonition:: Group Preamble — Centers from Bicentric Pairs, 1908-1982
   :class: note

   For a definition of a bicentric pair (e.g., the 1st and 2nd Brocard points) click **Tables** at the top of this page. Suppose P and U are a bicentric pair. Many operations on :g:`P <P>` and U result in :term:`triangle centers <triangle-center>`. Among these are trilinear and barycentric product, bicentric sum, bicentric difference, crosssum, and crossdifference. For definitions of these, click **Tables**. At the time this section is added to ETC (September 15, 2003), bicentric pairs

   P(1),U(1); P(2),U(2); ...; P(42),U(42)

   are defined in **Tables**. In this present section, the abbreviation **PU(n)** means the bicentric pair P(n),U(n).

.. _preamble-0028:

.. admonition:: Group Preamble — Orthocorrespondents, 1992-2006
   :class: note

   Suppose P is a point in the plane of triangle :g:`ABC << A B C >>`. The perpendiculars through :g:`P <P>` to the lines :g:`AP <[ A P ]>`, :g:`BP <[ B P ]>`, :g:`CP <[ C P ]>` meet the lines :g:`BC <[ B C ]>`, :g:`CA <[ C A ]>`, :g:`AB <[ A B ]>`, respectively, in collinear points. Let :g:`L <L>` denote their line. The trilinear pole of :g:`L <L>` is the *orthocorrespondent of :g:`P* <P*>`. This definition is introduced in :person:`Bernard Gibert <bernard-gibert>` , Orthocorrespondence and Orthopivotal Cubics, :cite:`Forum Geometricorum <forum-geometricorum>` 3 (2003) pages 1-27.

   | If P is given in barycentrics by :g:`P <P>` =\ :math:`p : q : r`, then the orthocorrespondent of P has barycentrics
   | :math:`f(a,b,c) : f(b,c,a) : f(c,a,b)`, where

   :math:`f(a,b,c) = a^{2}qr + (-pS_{A} + qS_{B} + rS_{C})p`,

   where :math:`S_{A}` = (:math:`b^{2}` + :math:`c^{2}` - :math:`a^{2})`/2, and :math:`S_{B}` and :math:`S_{C}` are defined cyclically.

   | If follows that if :g:`X <X>` =\ :math:`x : y : z` in trilinears, then the orthocorrespondent of X has trilinears
   | :math:`g(a,b,c) : g(b,c,a) : g(c,a,b)`, where

   :math:`g(a,b,c) = yz + (-x \cos A + y \cos B + z \cos C)x`.

   | Pairs (i,j) for which the orthocorrespondent of X_i is X_j include the following:
   | (1,57), (4,2), (11,651), (13,13), (14,14), (15,62), (16,61), (30,2), (98,287), (100,1332), (101,1331), (103,1815), (105,1814), (106,1797), (107,648), (108,651), (109,1813), (111,895), (112,110), (115,110), (125,648), (132,287), (1560,895), (1566,677), (1785,57)

   The orthocorrespondent of every point on the line at infinity is the centroid. Two *orthoassociate points* (i.e., an inverse pair in the polar circle, such as :ref:`X_112 <X_112>` and :ref:`X_115 <X_115>`) share the same orthocorrespondent.
