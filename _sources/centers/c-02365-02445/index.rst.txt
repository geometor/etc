X_2365–X_2445
=============

.. rubric:: Circumcircle CIR(X) Points & Gibert-Simson Transforms

.. enunciation::

   Circumcevian-eigentransform points CIR(X) on the circumcircle and their :term:`isogonal conjugates <isogonal-conjugate>` (:ref:`X_2365 <X_2365>`\ –\ :ref:`X_2393 <X_2393>`), followed by Gibert-Simson transforms GS(X) on the Simson cubic K010 (:ref:`X_2394 <X_2394>`\ –\ :ref:`X_2419 <X_2419>`) and their isogonal conjugates on K162 (:ref:`X_2420 <X_2420>`\ –\ :ref:`X_2445 <X_2445>`).

.. admonition:: Group Preamble — More Points on the Circumcircle, 2365-2384
   :class: note

   Suppose that :g:`U <U>` =\ :math:`u : v : w` (trilinears) is a :term:`triangle center <triangle-center>` other than :ref:`X_6 <X_6>`, and let CIR(U) be the point given by trilinears

   :math:`F(u,v,w) : F(v,w,u) : F(w,u,v)` = avw/(:math:`av^{2}` + :math:`aw^{2}` - buv - cuw) : bwu/(:math:`bw^{2}` + :math:`bu^{2}` - cvw - avu) : cuv/(:math:`cu^{2}` + :math:`cv^{2}` - awu - bwv)

   CIR(U) lies on the circumcircle. Pairs (i,j) such that X_j = CIR(X_i) include the following:

   (1,106), (2,729), (3,1300), (4,1294), (9,1477), (31,767), (37,741), (41,767), (43,106), (44,106), (74,1294), (75,701), (110,99), (125,827), (194,729), (238,741), (265,1300), (1084,689), (1154,1141), (1279,(1477), (1499,1296), (1503,1297), (1510,930), (1634,689)

   This section was added to ETC on 10/20/03; the preamble was revised on January 20, 2015, following suggestions by Viktor Kitaysky. Points with trilinears similar to those of CIR(U), called circum-eigentransforms, denoted by CET(U), are given in Section 4.

.. _preamble-0038:

.. admonition:: Group Preamble — GIBERT-SIMSON TRANSFORMS, X_2394 -X_2419
   :class: note

   On October 19, 2003, :person:`Bernard Gibert <bernard-gibert>` contributed points that lie on the Simson cubic. Indeed, this cubic is the image of the circumcircle under a mapping here named the Gibert-Simson transform. If :g:`U <U>` =\ :math:`u : v : w` (barycentrics) and U is not :ref:`X_3 <X_3>`, then the transform is given by

   GS(U) = (:math:`b^{2}`\ :math:`S_{B}`/v - :math:`c^{2}`\ :math:`S_{C}`/w)u/:math:`a^{2}` : (:math:`c^{2}`\ :math:`S_{C}`/w - :math:`a^{2}`\ :math:`S_{A}`/u)v/:math:`b^{2}` : (:math:`a^{2}`\ :math:`S_{A}`/u - :math:`b^{2}`\ :math:`S_{B}`/v)w/:math:`c^{2}`.

   In case U lies on the circumcircle, these barycentrics are proportional to the respective directed distances from :g:`A <A>`, :g:`B <B>`, :g:`C <C>`, to the Simson line of U. If :g:`U* <U*>` is the antipode of :g:`U <U>`, then GS(:g:`U* <U*>`) is the :term:`isotomic conjugate <isotomic-conjugate>` of GS(U). In the Catalogue at Cubics in the Triangle Plane, the Simson cubic is discussed as K010, and its isogonal transform is the cubic `K162 <http://perso.wanadoo.fr/bernard.gibert/ctc.html>`__. The isogonal conjugates of centers :ref:`X_2365 <X_2365>`-:ref:`X_2419 <X_2419>` are indexed as :ref:`X_2420 <X_2420>`-:ref:`X_2445 <X_2445>`.

   If :g:`U <U>` =\ :math:`u : v : w` =\ :math:`u(a,b,c) : u(b,c,a) : u(c,a,b)` (trilinears), then

   | GS(U) =\ :math:`f(a,b,c) : f(b,c,a) : f(c,a,b)`, where :math:`f(a,b,c) = (v \cos C - w \cos B)(u/a)^{2}`,
   | and the cubic K010 is given by trilinears

   (cos A)\ :math:`x(b^{2}`\ :math:`y^{2}` + :math:`c^{2}`\ :math:`z^{2})` + (cos B)\ :math:`y(c^{2}`\ :math:`z^{2}` + :math:`a^{2}`\ :math:`x^{2})` + (cos C)\ :math:`z(a^{2}`\ :math:`x^{2}` + :math:`b^{2}`\ :math:`x^{2})` - (:math:`a^{2}` + :math:`b^{2}` + :math:`c^{2})`\ xyz = 0,

   and the cubic K162 by

   (cos A)\ :math:`x(c^{2}`\ :math:`y^{2}` + :math:`b^{2}`\ :math:`z^{2})` + (cos B)\ :math:`y(a^{2}`\ :math:`z^{2}` + :math:`c^{2}`\ :math:`x^{2})` + (cos C)\ :math:`z(b^{2}`\ :math:`x^{2}` + :math:`a^{2}`\ :math:`x^{2})` - (:math:`a^{2}` + :math:`b^{2}` + :math:`c^{2})`\ xyz = 0,

   In the definition of eigentransform before :ref:`X_2120 <X_2120>`, if **cevian triangle** is replaced by **circumcevian triangle**, the result is CET(U) as discussed before :ref:`X_2365 <X_2365>`. Gibert observed that if **cevian triangle** is replaced by **pedal triangle, for U on the circumcircle**, the result is the isogonal conjugate of GS(U). That is, [GS(U)] :sup:`-1` is the :term:`eigencenter <eigencenter>` of the :term:`pedal triangle <pedal-triangle>` of U (which is a degenerate triangle lying in the Simson line of :g:`U <U>`).

   Regarding triangle centers that do not lie on the circumcircle, GS(X_i) = X_j for these (i,j): (32,669), (48,1459), (187,1649), (248,879), (485,850), (486,850).

   This section was added to ETC on 10/23/03. For more Gibert-Simson transforms, see X_15411-X_15423
