X_371
=====

.. rubric:: KENMOTU POINT (CONGRUENT SQUARES POINT)

.. enunciation::

   There exist three congruent squares :g:`U <U>`, :g:`V <V>`, :g:`W <W>` positioned in :g:`ABC << A B C >>` as follows: U has opposing vertices on segments :g:`AB <[ A B ]>` and :g:`AC <[ A C ]>`; V has opposing vertices on segments :g:`BC <[ B C ]>` and :g:`BA <[ B A ]>`; W has opposing vertices on segments :g:`CA <[ C A ]>` and :g:`CB <[ C B ]>`, and there is a single point common to :g:`U <U>`, :g:`V <V>`, :g:`W <W>`. The common point, :ref:`X_371 <X_371>`, may have first been published in Kenmotu's *Collection of Sangaku Problems* in 1840, indicating that its first appearance may have been anonymously inscribed on a wooden board hung up in a Japanese shrine or temple. (The Kenmotu configuration uses only half-squares; i.e., isosceles right triangles). Trilinears were found by John Rigby.

.. container:: note-item

   The edgelength of the three squares is :math:`2^{1/2}`\ abc/(:math:`a^{2}` + :math:`b^{2}` + :math:`c^{2}` + 4σ), where σ = area(:g:`ABC << A B C >>`). (Edward Brisse, 2/12/2000)

.. container:: note-item

   :ref:`X_371 <X_371>` is the internal center of similitude of the circumcircle and the 2nd Lemoine circle (cosine circle) (Peter J. C. Moses, 5/9/03). Also, :ref:`X_371 <X_371>` is the internal center of similitude of the Gallatly circle (defined just before X_2007) and the 1st Lemoine circle (Peter J. C. Moses, 9/10/2003).

.. container:: note-item

   :ref:`X_371 <X_371>` is the perspector of several pairs of triangles associated with Lucas circles. Some of these triangles are defined at MathWorld; e.g. `Lucas Central Triangle <http://mathworld.wolfram.com/LucasCentralTriangle.html>`__, and others are Lucas(L:W) configurations. The latter are generalizations of configurations associated with Lucas circles, in which the squares are replaced by rectangles of length-to-width ratio L:W, with length on the corresponding sideline of :g:`ABC << A B C >>`. A negative ratio indicates that the rectangles are directed inward; e.g. Lucas(-1:1) indicates inward-directed squares, whereas Lucas(1:1) indicates the classical case of outward-directed squares. These generalizations and the following properties of :ref:`X_371 <X_371>` were contributed by Randy Hutson, 9/23/2011. See also :ref:`X_372 <X_372>` and X_1084.

.. container:: note-item

   **Hidetoshi Fukagawa and John F. Rigby,** *Traditional Japanese Mathematics Problems of the 18th and 19th Centuries,* SCT Publishing, Singapore, 2002. Reviewed, together with the Fukagawa and Pedoe book, *Japanese Tempe Geometry Problems: San Gaku,* by :person:`Clark Kimberling <clark-kimberling>` in *The Mathematical Intelligencer* 28, no. 1 (Winter 2006) 61-63.

.. container:: note-item

   Let :g:`A'B'C' << A' B' C' >>` be the Lucas central triangle. Let :g:`A″ <A″>` be the cevapoint of :g:`B' <B'>` and :g:`C' <C'>`, and define :g:`B″ <B″>` and :g:`C″ <C″>` cyclically. The lines :g:`AA″ <[ A A″ ]>`, :g:`BB″ <[ B B″ ]>`, :g:`CC″ <[ C C″ ]>` concur in :ref:`X_371 <X_371>`. (Randy Hutson, July 23, 2015)
