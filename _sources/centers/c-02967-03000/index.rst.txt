.. _preamble-0044:

.. admonition:: Group Preamble — MacBeath Points
   :class: note

   The MacBeath inconic is the conic inscribed in triangle :g:`ABC << A B C >>` having center :ref:`X_5 <X_5>` and foci :ref:`X_3 <X_3>` and :ref:`X_4 <X_4>`. An equation for this conic in barycentric coordinates is

   :math:`u^{2}`\ :math:`x^{2}` + :math:`v^{2}`\ :math:`y^{2}` + :math:`w^{2}`\ :math:`z^{2}` - 2wvyz - 2wuzx - 2uvxy = 0,

   where (u, v, w) = (sin 2A, sin 2B, sin 2C), i.e.,\ :math:`u : v : w` = :ref:`X_3 <X_3>`.

   In the parlance of :cite:`TCCT <tcct>`, page 238, the MacBeath inconic is W(:ref:`X_184 <X_184>`), with trilinear equation

   :math:`U^{2}`\ :math:`x^{2}` + :math:`V^{2}`\ :math:`y^{2}` + :math:`W^{2}`\ :math:`z^{2}` - 2WVyz - 2WUzx - 2UVxy = 0,

   where :math:`U : V : W` = :ref:`X_184 <X_184>` = :math:`a^{2}`\ cos A : :math:`b^{2}`\ cos B : :math:`c^{2}`\ cos C.

   Points lying on the MacBeath inconic were contributed by :person:`Peter Moses <peter-moses>` (:cite:`Hyacinthos <hyacinthos>`, Nov. 12, 2004), and a selection of these are given here as :ref:`X_2967 <X_2967>` to :ref:`X_2973 <X_2973>`. Comments written by Alexander Murray MacBeath are given in a :cite:`Hyacinthos <hyacinthos>` message of Dec. 12, 2004 from Antreas P. Hatzipolakis. See also `MathWorld <http://mathworld.wolfram.com/MacBeathInconic.html>`__ and more than a dozen :cite:`Hyacinthos <hyacinthos>` messages numbered between 10870 and 10923.

   Macbeath authored articles entitled "The Deltoid I," The Deltoid II," and "The Deltoid III," in *Eureka*. In the second of these, 11 (1949) 26-29, he introduces the conic as follows:

   Lemma 1: Let :g:`O <O>`, :g:`H <H>` be the common points of a coaxal system of circles. Let a variable circle of the system cut the line of centres at C. Let :g:`T <T>` be a point on the circumference such that TC=k\*\ :g:`OC <[ O C ]>`, where k is a fixed ratio. Then the locus of :g:`T <T>` is a conic with foci at :g:`O <O>`,H. In :cite:`Hyacinthos <hyacinthos>` message 2009, dated Dec. 9, 2000, John Conway wrote This I know as "the MacBeath ellipse", since Murray MacBeath wrote a nice article about it in "Eureka", the Cambridge University undergraduate mathematical journal. Thus did the conic get its name. Points :ref:`X_339 <X_339>`, :ref:`X_1312 <X_1312>`, and :ref:`X_1313 <X_1313>` lie on the MacBeath inconic. Peter Moses (Nov. 12, 2004) noted that if X is on this conic, then the reflection of :g:`X <X>` in :ref:`X_5 <X_5>`, and also the reflection of :g:`X <X>` in the Euler line, lie on the conic. He introduced the MacBeath circumconic, dual to the MacBeath inconic. The circumconic is given in barycentrics by

   (sin 2A)yz + (sin 2B)zx + (sin 2C)xy = 0,

   and in trilinears by

   (cos A)yz + (cos B)zx + (cos C)xy = 0,

   and it passes through X_i for I = 110, 287, 648, 651, 677, 895, 1331, 1332, 1797, 1813, 1814, 1815.

.. _preamble-0045:

.. admonition:: Group Preamble — Dual triangles, DC and CD Points
   :class: note

   Suppose :g:`DEF << D E F >>` is a triangle in the plane of triangle :g:`ABC << A B C >>`. Let :g:`D' <D'>` be the :term:`isogonal conjugate <isogonal-conjugate>` of the point of intersection of line :g:`EF <[ E F ]>` and the line at infinity. Define :g:`E' <E'>` and :g:`F' <F'>` cyclically. The triangle :g:`D'E'F' << D' E' F' >>` is here named the *dual* of :g:`DEF << D E F >>`. The vertices of :g:`D'E'F' << D' E' F' >>` lie on the circumcircle, and :g:`D'E'F' << D' E' F' >>` is similar to :g:`DEF << D E F >>`. The duality is between the sidelines :g:`EF <[ E F ]>`, :g:`FD <[ F D ]>`, :g:`DE <[ D E ]>` and the points :g:`D' <D'>`, :g:`E' <E'>`, :g:`F' <F'>`, respectively. For example, if :g:`E <E>` and :g:`F <F>` remain fixed and D varies, then :g:`D' <D'>` remains fixed, while :g:`E'F' <[ E' F' ]>` varies. Actually :g:`D'E'F' << D' E' F' >>` is the dual of any triangle homothetic to :g:`DEF << D E F >>`. Indeed, :g:`DEF << D E F >>` need not be a triangle but can be the union of three concurrent lines. (Proof of similarity follows from Theorem 6E in :cite:`TCCT <tcct>`, as the "gamma triangle" there is the dual of a triangle whose sidelines are respectively perpendicular to those of :g:`DEF << D E F >>`.)

   Suppose U is a point having :term:`cevian triangle <cevian-triangle>` :g:`DEF << D E F >>` and dual triangle :g:`D'E'F' << D' E' F' >>`. Then there exists a point DC(U) whose circum-:term:`anticevian triangle <anticevian-triangle>` (:cite:`TCCT <tcct>`, p. 201) is :g:`D'E'F' << D' E' F' >>`. The mapping :g:`DC <[ D C ]>` is given for :g:`U <U>` =\ :math:`u : v : w` (trilinears) by

   DC(U) = vw/(bv + cw) : wu/(cw + au) : uv/(au + bv).

   To construct DC(U) from :g:`U <U>`, let :g:`A' <A'>` = :g:`AD' <[ A D' ]>`\ ∩\ :g:`BC <[ B C ]>`, and let :g:`A″ <A″>` be the {B,C}-harmonic conjugate of :g:`A' <A'>`. Define :g:`B″ <B″>` and :g:`C″ <C″>` cyclically. The lines :g:`AA″ <[ A A″ ]>`, :g:`BB″ <[ B B″ ]>`, :g:`CC″ <[ C C″ ]>` concur in DC(U). Also, DC(U) = U-:term:`isoconjugate <isoconjugate>` of the :term:`crosssum <crosssum>` of :g:`U <U>` and :ref:`X_6 <X_6>`.

   If you have The Geometer's Sketchpad, you can view **DUAL TRIANGLE** (``DualTriangle.gsp``).

   Inversely, the circum-anticevian triangle of a point :g:`P <P>` is the dual of the cevian triangle of a point CD(P), given for :g:`P <P>` =\ :math:`p : q : r` by the inverse of the :g:`DC <[ D C ]>`-mapping; that is:

   CD(P) = bc/(-a/p + b/q + c/r) : ca/(a/p - b/q + c/r) : ab/(a/p + b/q - c/r) = isogonal conjugate of :ref:`X_6 <X_6>`-:term:`Ceva conjugate <ceva-conjugate>` of :g:`P <P>`.

   The appearance of (i,j) in the following list means that X_j = CD(X_i):

   1,7 2,4 3,253 4,69 6,2 9,189 31,330 37,1029 39,1031 54,264 57,8 58,75 59,693 74,1494, etc. (Just transpose the pairs in the list for DC(U).)

   The vertices of the dual :g:`D'E'F' << D' E' F' >>` of the cevian triangle of a point :g:`U <U>` =\ :math:`u : v : w` are given by

   | :g:`D' <D'>` = 1/(b/w - c/v) : -1/(c/u + a/w) : 1/(a/v + b/u)
   | :g:`E' <E'>` = 1/(b/w + c/v) : 1/(c/u - a/w) : -1/(a/v + b/u)
   | :g:`F' <F'>` = -1/(b/w + c/v) : 1/(c/u + a/w) : 1/(a/v - b/u).

   Triangle :g:`D'E'F' << D' E' F' >>` can be obtained from :g:`DEF << D E F >>` by translation, dilation, and rotation. First translate :g:`DEF << D E F >>` by vector :g:`O'O <[ O' O ]>`, where :g:`O' <O'>` = circumcenter of :g:`D'E'F' << D' E' F' >>` and :g:`O <O>` = circumcenter of :g:`ABC << A B C >>`. Then dilate :g:`D'E'F' << D' E' F' >>` so that its circumradius is that of :g:`ABC << A B C >>`. Then rotate :g:`D'E'F' << D' E' F' >>` so that angle AOD' is twice the angle between lines :g:`BC <[ B C ]>` and :g:`EF <[ E F ]>`.
