Notes & Constructions
---------------------

.. container:: note-item

   As a point on the Euler line, :ref:`X_3 <X_3>` has Shinagawa coefficients :math:`(1, -1)`.

.. container:: note-item

   The tangents at vertices of :term:`excentral triangle <excentral-triangle>` to the McCay cubic K003 concur in :ref:`X_3 <X_3>`. Also, the tangents at :g:`A <A>`, :g:`B <B>`, :g:`C <C>` to the orthocubic K006 concur in :ref:`X_3 <X_3>`. (:person:`Randy Hutson <randy-hutson>`, November 18, 2015)

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``H_aH_bH_c`` be the :term:`cevian triangle <cevian-triangle>` of :ref:`X_4 <X_4>`. Let ``A″`` be :ref:`X_4 <X_4>`-of-``AH_bH_c``, and define ``B″``, ``C″`` cyclically. The lines ``AA″``, ``BB″``, ``CC″`` concur in :ref:`X_3 <X_3>`. (Randy Hutson, June 27, 2018)

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``P`` be a point in the plane of :g:`ABC << A B C >>`. Let ``P'`` be the :term:`isogonal conjugate <isogonal-conjugate>` of P. Let ``P″`` be the pedal antipodal perspector of ``P``. :ref:`X_3 <X_3>` is the unique point ``P`` for which ``P'`` = ``P″``. (Randy Hutson, June 27, 2018)

.. container:: note-item

   Taking a reference triangle :g:`ABC << A B C >>` and a variable point ``P`` on the plane, ``P``\ =\ :ref:`X_3 <X_3>` is the point of maximal area of its :term:`pedal triangle <pedal-triangle>` when considering all points P inside the circumcircle of :g:`ABC << A B C >>`. There are points P far away from the circumcircle for which the area of their pedal triangles is much larger. However, if you consider the signed area of the pedal triangle of P (of which sign depends on whether the points are in clockwise or anti-clockwise order), you could just say that the area of the pedal triangle of ``P`` is always negative whenever P is outside of the circumcircle so that ``P``\ =\ :ref:`X_3 <X_3>` gives the global maximum. (Mark Helman, July 10, 2020)

.. container:: note-item

   A slightly similar thing happens regarding the area of the :term:`antipedal triangle <antipedal-triangle>` of ``P``. ``P``\ =\ :ref:`X_4 <X_4>` has the smallest area of its antipedal amongst all P in the interior of triangle :g:`ABC << A B C >>` (when :ref:`X_4 <X_4>` is in this interior). There are points P (on the circumcircle) for which this area goes to 0. However, if we consider the signed area of the antipedal, even though there are still regions of the plane outside of :g:`ABC << A B C >>` where the signed area is positive, ``P``\ =\ :ref:`X_4 <X_4>` gives the smallest area of the antipedal among all P for which this area is positive (this works even when :g:`ABC << A B C >>` is obtuse, and points close to the circumcircle (on both sides) have negative antipedal area). (Mark Helman, July 10, 2020)

.. container:: note-item

   View `Extremal Area Pedal and Antipedal Triangles <https://dan-reznik.github.io/extremal-pedal-antipedal/>`__, by Mark Helman, Ronaldo Garcia, and :person:`Dan Reznik <dan-reznik>`.

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``T`` be any one of these trianges: {Aries, :ref:`X_3 <X_3>`-Ehrmann, X3-:g:`ABC << A B C >>` reflections, 3rd pedal of :ref:`X_3 <X_3>`, 3rd antipedal of :ref:`X_3 <X_3>`, inner-Le Viet An, outer-Le Viet An}. Let ``O_A`` be the circle centered at the :g:`A <A>`-vertex of ``T`` and passing through :g:`A <A>`; define ``O_B``\ and ``O_C`` cyclically. :ref:`X_3 <X_3>` is the radical center of ``O_A``, ``O_B``, ``O_C``. (Randy Hutson, August 30, 2020)

.. container:: note-item

   In the plane of a triangle :g:`ABC << A B C >>`, let

      * ``O_a`` = circle with diameter :g:`BC <[ B C ]>`, and define ``O_b`` and ``O_c`` cyclically;
      * ``A'`` = reflection of :g:`A <A>` in ``O_a``, and define ``B'`` and ``C'`` cyclically;
      * ``A_b`` = ``BA'``\ ∩\ ``O_a``, and define ``B_c`` and ``C_a`` cyclically;
      * ``A_c`` = ``CA'``\ ∩\ ``O_a``, and define ``B_a`` and ``C_b`` cyclically;
      * ``A_1`` = ``B_cB_a``\ ∩\ ``C_aC_b``, and define ``B_1`` and ``C_1`` cyclically.

   The triangle ``A_1B_1C_1`` is perspective to :g:`ABC << A B C >>`, and the perspector is :ref:`X_3 <X_3>`. (:person:`Dasari Naga Vijay Krishna <dasari-naga-vijay-krishna>`, April 19, 2021)

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``O`` be a point (not necessarily :ref:`X_3 <X_3>`), and let be a ``AOB`` be a fixed-angle sector of a circle :g:`C <C>`\ =(O,R), rigidly rotating about center O. Let ``P`` be an arbitrary point. The locus of :ref:`X_3 <X_3>`-of-``PAB`` is a conic E whose major axis is ``OP``. This conic is an ellipse (resp. hyperbola) if P is interior (resp. exterior) to C. One of its foci is O. `Figure (ellipse) <https://user-images.githubusercontent.com/16231062/145676689-1e52d8af-272b-403c-9f6f-8e9de14e192e.png>`__. `Figure (hyperbola) <https://user-images.githubusercontent.com/16231062/145676790-e5b5e328-5498-4a52-94d0-61769a695355.png>`__. (Dan Reznik, December 10, 2021)

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``A'B'C'`` be the :term:`anticevian triangle <anticevian-triangle>` of :ref:`X_3 <X_3>`, and let ``E_a`` be the ellipse passing through ``A'`` and having foci ``B'`` and ``C'``. Define ``E_b`` and ``E_c`` cyclically. The 6 major vertices of the three ellipses lie on a circle that is concentric with the circumcircle of ``A'B'C'``. `Figure <https://user-images.githubusercontent.com/16231062/146693684-554598a6-66c6-4600-9791-db33a8877ada.png>`__. (Dan Reznik, December 19, 2021)

.. container:: note-item

   :ref:`X_3 <X_3>` is the {:ref:`X_2 <X_2>`,\ :ref:`X_4 <X_4>`}-harmonic conjugate of :ref:`X_5 <X_5>`. If triangle :g:`ABC << A B C >>` is acute, then :ref:`X_3 <X_3>` is the incenter of the :term:`tangential triangle <tangential-triangle>` and the Bevan point, :ref:`X_40 <X_40>`, of the :term:`orthic triangle <orthic-triangle>`.
