Notes & Constructions
---------------------

.. container:: note-item

   As a point on the Euler line, :ref:`X_4 <X_4>` has Shinagawa coefficients :math:`(0, 1)`.

.. container:: note-item

   The tangents at :g:`A <A>`, :g:`B <B>`, :g:`C <C>` to the McCay cubic K003 concur in :ref:`X_4 <X_4>`. Also, the tangents at :g:`A <A>`, :g:`B <B>`, :g:`C <C>` to the Lucas cubic K007 concur in :ref:`X_4 <X_4>`. (:person:`Randy Hutson <randy-hutson>`, November 18, 2015)

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``P`` be a point in the plane of :g:`ABC << A B C >>`. Let ``O_a`` be the circumcenter of ``BCP``, and define ``O_b`` and ``O_c`` cyclically. Let ``Q`` be the circumcenter of ``O_aO_bO_c``. ``P`` = Q only when ``P`` = :ref:`X_4 <X_4>`. (Randy Hutson, June 27, 2018)

.. container:: note-item

   Taking a reference triangle :g:`ABC << A B C >>` and a variable point ``P`` on the plane, ``P``\ =\ :ref:`X_3 <X_3>` is the point of maximal area of its :term:`pedal triangle <pedal-triangle>` when considering all points P inside the circumcircle of :g:`ABC << A B C >>`. There are points P far away from the circumcircle for which the area of their pedal triangles is much larger. However, if you consider the signed area of the pedal triangle of P (of which sign depends on whether the points are in clockwise or anti-clockwise order), you could just say that the area of the pedal triangle of ``P`` is always negative whenever P is outside of the circumcircle so that ``P``\ =\ :ref:`X_3 <X_3>` gives the global maximum. (Mark Helman, July 10, 2020)

.. container:: note-item

   A slightly similar thing happens regarding the area of the :term:`antipedal triangle <antipedal-triangle>` of ``P``. ``P``\ =\ :ref:`X_4 <X_4>` has the smallest area of its antipedal amongst all P in the interior of triangle :g:`ABC << A B C >>` (when :ref:`X_4 <X_4>` is in this interior). There are points P (on the circumcircle) for which this area goes to 0. However, if we consider the signed area of the antipedal, even though there are still regions of the plane outside of :g:`ABC << A B C >>` where the signed area is positive, ``P``\ =\ :ref:`X_4 <X_4>` gives the smallest area of the antipedal among all P for which this area is positive (this works even when :g:`ABC << A B C >>` is obtuse, and points close to the circumcircle (on both sides) have negative antipedal area). (Mark Helman, July 10, 2020)

.. container:: note-item

   View `Extremal Area Pedal and Antipedal Triangles <https://dan-reznik.github.io/extremal-pedal-antipedal/>`__, by Mark Helman, Ronaldo Garcia, and :person:`Dan Reznik <dan-reznik>`.

.. container:: note-item

   :ref:`X_4 <X_4>` and the vertices :g:`A <A>`, :g:`B <B>`, :g:`C <C>` comprise an orthocentric system, defined as a set of four points, one of which is the orthocenter of the triangle of the other three (so that each is the orthocenter of the other three). The incenter and excenters also form an orthocentric system. If :g:`ABC << A B C >>` is acute, then :ref:`X_4 <X_4>` is the incenter of the :term:`orthic triangle <orthic-triangle>`.

.. container:: note-item

   Suppose P is not on a sideline of :g:`ABC << A B C >>`. Let ``A'B'C'`` be the :term:`cevian triangle <cevian-triangle>` of P. Let ``D``, ``E``, ``F`` be the circles having diameters ``AA'``, ``BB'``,\ ``CC'``. The radical center of ``D``, ``E``, ``F`` is :ref:`X_4 <X_4>`. (:person:`Floor van Lamoen <floor-van-lamoen>`, :cite:`Hyacinthos #214 <hyacinthos>`, Jan. 24, 2000.)

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``A_2B_2C_2`` be the 2nd Conway triangle. Let ``A'`` be the :term:`crosspoint <crosspoint>` of ``B_2`` and ``C_2``, and define ``B'`` and ``C'`` cyclically. The lines ``AA'``, ``BB'``, ``CC'`` concur in :ref:`X_4 <X_4>`. (Randy Hutson, December 10, 2016)

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``T`` be any one of these trianges: {anticevian of :ref:`X_30 <X_30>`, anti-Hutson-intouch, anti-incircle-circles, Ehrmann side, :ref:`X_2 <X_2>`-Ehrmann, Gemini 15, Gemini 16, Kosnita, midheight, N-obverse of :ref:`X_6 <X_6>`, Schroeter, tangential, Trinh, 1st Zaniah, 2nd Zaniah}. Let ``O_A`` be the circle centered at the :g:`A <A>`-vertex of ``T`` and passing through :g:`A <A>`; define ``O_B`` and ``O_C`` cyclically. :ref:`X_4 <X_4>` is the radical center of ``O_A``, ``O_B``, ``O_C``. (Randy Hutson, August 30, 2020)

.. container:: note-item

   See **Ross Honsberger,** *Episodes in Nineteenth and Twentieth Century Euclidean Geometry,* Mathematical Association of America, 1995. Chapter 2: The Orthocenter.

.. container:: note-item

   In the plane of a triangle :g:`ABC << A B C >>`, let

      * ``O_a`` = circle with diameter :g:`BC <[ B C ]>`, and define ``O_b`` and ``O_c`` cyclically;
      * ``A'`` = reflection of :g:`A <A>` in ``O_a``, and define ``B'`` and ``C'`` cyclically;
      * ``A_b`` = polar of ``A'`` with respect to ``O_b``, and define ``B_c`` and ``C_a`` cyclically;
      * ``A_c`` = polar of ``A'`` with respect to ``OC``, and define ``B_a`` and ``C_b`` cyclically;
      * ″ = ``A_b``\ ∩\ ``A_c``, and define ``B″`` and ``C″`` cyclically.

   The triangle ``A″B″C″`` is perspective to :g:`ABC << A B C >>`, and the perspector is :ref:`X_4 <X_4>`. (:person:`Dasari Naga Vijay Krishna <dasari-naga-vijay-krishna>`, April 19, 2021)

.. container:: note-item

   In the plane of a triangle :g:`ABC << A B C >>`, let

      * ``O_a`` = circle with diameter :g:`BC <[ B C ]>`, and define ``O_b`` and ``O_c`` cyclically;
      * ``P_a`` = polar of :g:`A <A>` with respect to ``O_a``, and define ``P_b`` and ``P_c`` cyclically.

   Then :ref:`X_4 <X_4>`\ = ``P_a``\ ∩\ ``P_b``\ ∩\ ``P_c``, (Dasari Naga Vijay Krishna, July 19, 2021)
