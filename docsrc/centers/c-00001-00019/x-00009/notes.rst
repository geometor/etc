Notes & Constructions
---------------------

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``A'`` be the orthocorrespondent of the :g:`A <A>`-excenter, and define ``B'`` and ``C'`` cyclically. The lines ``AA'``, ``BB'``, ``CC'`` concur in :ref:`X_9 <X_9>`. (:person:`Randy Hutson <randy-hutson>`, November 18, 2015)

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``H_aH_bH_c`` be the :term:`orthic triangle <orthic-triangle>`. Let ``L_a`` be the antiorthic axis of ``AH_bH_c``, and define ``L_b`` and ``L_c`` cyclically. Let ``A″`` = ``L_b``\ ∩\ ``L_c``. ``B″`` = ``L_c``\ ∩\ ``L_a``, ``C″`` = ``L_a``\ ∩\ ``L_b``. Triangle ``A″B″C″`` is inversely similar to :g:`ABC << A B C >>`, with similitude center :ref:`X_9 <X_9>`. (Randy Hutson, November 18, 2015)

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``E`` be the locus of the :term:`trilinear pole <trilinear-pole>` of a line that passes through :ref:`X_1 <X_1>`. The center of ``E`` is :ref:`X_9 <X_9>`. Moreover, E passes through the points :ref:`X_100 <X_100>`, :ref:`X_658 <X_658>`, :ref:`X_662 <X_662>`, :ref:`X_799 <X_799>`, :ref:`X_1821 <X_1821>`, :ref:`X_2580 <X_2580>`, :ref:`X_2581 <X_2581>` and the bicentric pairs PU(34), PU(75), PU(77), PU(79). Also, E is a circumellipse of :g:`ABC << A B C >>` and an inellipse of the :term:`excentral triangle <excentral-triangle>`. (Randy Hutson, February 10, 2016)

.. container:: note-item

   The locus E also passes through the vertices of Gemini triangle 2. (Randy Hutson, November 30, 2018)

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``A'`` be the intersection of the tangents to the :g:`A <A>`-excircle at the intercepts with the circumcircle, and define ``B'`` and ``C'`` cyclically. The lines ``AA'``, ``BB'``, ``CC'`` concur in :ref:`X_9 <X_9>`. (Randy Hutson, December 2, 2017)

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``A'`` be the perspector of the :g:`A <A>`-mixtilinear excircle, and define ``B'`` and ``C'`` cyclically. The lines ``AA'``, ``BB'``, ``CC'`` concur in :ref:`X_9 <X_9>`. (Randy Hutson, December 2, 2017)

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``E`` be the circumellipse of ``T`` = :g:`ABC << A B C >>` with center :ref:`X_9 <X_9>`. Then :g:`ABC << A B C >>` is a billiard orbit of E (3-periodic). If we fix E in the plane, all its triangular orbits (a set of "rotating" triangles ``T``) have the same :ref:`X_9 <X_9>`. Note that :ref:`X_9 <X_9>` is the point of concurrence of lines drawn from each excenter to the midpoint of the corresponding side of ``T``. (:person:`Dan Reznik <dan-reznik>`, June 30, 2019) See [1] `Triangular Orbits in Elliptic Billiards: the Mittenpunkt X(9) is stationary at the origin <https://youtu.be/tMrBqfRBYik>`__ and [2] `Triangular Orbits in Elliptic Billiards: the Mittenpunkt X(9) is stationary at the origin <https://dan-reznik.github.io/Elliptical-Billiards-Triangular-Orbits/>`__.

.. container:: note-item

   NEW in 2020: A particularly fine article is recommended: ' Can the Ellliptic Billiard Still Surprise Us? ', by Dan Reznik, Ronaldo Garcia, and Jair Koiller , in Mathematical Intelligencer 42 (2020) 6-17. An online pdf is available: `Click here. <https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01515>`__

.. container:: note-item

   Notes from :person:`Peter Moses <peter-moses>` (July 1, 2019) about the circumellipse E with center :ref:`X_9 <X_9>`:

      * E passes through the vertices of these triangles: :g:`ABC << A B C >>`; Honsberger (see X_7670); Inner Conway (see X_11677); Gemini 2, Gemini 30.
      * E passes through the point X_i for these i:
      * 88, 100, 162, 190, 651, 653, 655, 658, 660, 662, 673, 771, 799,
      * 823, 897, 1156, 1492, 1821, 2349, 2580, 2581, 3257, 4598, 4599
      * 4604, 4606, 4607, 8052, 20332, 23707, 24624, 27834, 32680.
      * The perspector of ``E`` is :ref:`X_1 <X_1>`; the major axis of E passes through X_i for these i: 9, 2590, 3307, 24646, and the minor axis, for these i: 9, 2591, 3309, 24647. The ellipse E is the :term:`isogonal conjugate <isogonal-conjugate>` of the antiorthic axis.
      * Let g = length of semi-major axis of ``E``; then g = Sqrt[(2 R (r + R + :math:`\sqrt{R(R - 2 r)}`) s^2)/(r + 4 R)^2].
      * Let h = length of semi-minor axis of ``E``: then h = Sqrt[(2 R (r + R - :math:`\sqrt{R(R - 2 r)}`) s^2)/(r + 4 R)^2].
      * h/g = :math:`\sqrt{(OI - R) (OI + 3 R) / ((OI - 3 R) (OI + R))}` = Sqrt[(r + R - :math:`\sqrt{R (R - 2 r)}`) / (r + R + :math:`\sqrt{R (R - 2 r)}`)].
      * eccentricity of ``E``: 2 :math:`\sqrt{OI R / ((3 R - OI) (OI + R))}`.
      * If F is a focus of ``E``, then \|F\ :ref:`X_9 <X_9>`\ \|^2 = 4 s^2 R :math:`\sqrt{R (R - 2 r)}` / (r + 4 R)^2/.
      * The axes of ``E`` are the asymptotes of the Feuerbach hyperbola.
      * The area of ``E`` is π R S /(r (r + 4 R))^(3/2) = 4 π a b c / ( 2 b c + 2 c a + 2 a b - a^2 - b^2 - c^2 )^(3/2) \* area(:g:`ABC << A B C >>`).

   The triangle tangent to the vertices of ``E`` is the excentral triangle.

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``A'B'C'`` be the :term:`intouch triangle <intouch-triangle>` of the :term:`anticomplementary triangle <anticomplementary-triangle>` of :g:`ABC << A B C >>`. The ellipse E passes through ``A'``, ``B'``, ``C'``. See `Three orbits in elliptic billiard <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gwfx6LDJnsE&feature=youtu.be>`__. (Dan Reznik, July 1, 2019)

.. container:: note-item

   The circumellipse with center :ref:`X_9 <X_9>` meets the circumhyperbola with center :ref:`X_11 <X_11>` (i.e., the Feuerbach hyperbola) in :ref:`X_1156 <X_1156>`. (Dan Reznik, January 20, 2020.)

.. container:: note-item

   Each of the following cubics passes through the four foci (two real and two imaginary) of the ellipse E: K040, K351, K352, K710, K716, K1060. The two real foci are a pair of isogonal conjugates, and likewise for the two imaginary foci. Moreover, if :math:`p : q : r` is on the circumcircle, then :math:`p/a : q/b : r/c` is on ``E``. (Peter Moses, July 2 and 3, 2019)

.. container:: note-item

   The ellipse E is the isogonal conjugate of the antiorthic axis, :ref:`X_44 <X_44>`\ \ :ref:`X_513 <X_513>`, with barycentric equation :math:`x + y + z = 0`, and E is the :term:`isotomic conjugate <isotomic-conjugate>` of the :ref:`X_514 <X_514>`\ \ :ref:`X_661 <X_661>`, with barycentric equation a x + b y + c z = 0. This line, denoted by L(31), is the perspectrix of the anticomplementary triangle and the inner Conway triangle (which is the intouch triangle of the anticomplementary triangle). Points lying on :ref:`X_514 <X_514>`\ \ :ref:`X_661 <X_661>` include X_i for i = 514, 661, 693, 857, 908, 914, 1577, 1959, 2084, 2582, 2583, 3239, 3250, 3762, 3766, 3835, 3904, 3912, 3936, 3948, 4129, 4358, 4391, 4462, 4468, 4486, 4728, 4766, 4776, 4789, 4791, 4801, 4823, 4978, 5074, 5179, 6332, 6381, 6590, 8045, 14206, 14207, 14208, 14209, 14210, 14281, 14349, 14350, 14963, 18669, 18715, 24018, 30565, 30566, 30804, 30806, 32679. (Peter Moses, July 2 and 3, 2019)
