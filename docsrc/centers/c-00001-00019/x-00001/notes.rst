   * - :g:`A <A>`-excenter
     - :math:`-1 : 1 : 1`

   * - :g:`B <B>`-excenter
     - :math:`1 : -1 : 1`

   * - :g:`C <C>`-excenter
     - :math:`1 : 1 : -1`
     - :math:`r_c = \frac{2\Delta}{a+b-c}`

.. container:: note-item

   The radii of the excircles are :math:`2 \operatorname{area}(ABC)/(-a + b + c)`, :math:`2 \operatorname{area}(ABC)/(a - b + c)`, :math:`2 \operatorname{area}(ABC)/(a + b - c)`.

.. container:: note-item

   Writing the A-exradius as r(a,b,c), the others are r(b,c,a) and r(c,a,b). If these exradii are abbreviated as :math:`r_{a}`, :math:`r_{b}`, :math:`r_{c}`, then :math:`1/r = 1/r_{a} +1/r_{b} + 1/r_{c}`. Moreover,

   :math:`\operatorname{area}(ABC) = \sqrt{r r_{a} r_{b} r_{c}}` and :math:`r_{a} + r_{b} + r_{c} = r + 4R`, where R denotes the radius of the circumcircle.

.. container:: note-item

   The incenter is the identity of the group of :term:`triangle centers <triangle-center>` under **trilinear multiplication** defined by :math:`(x : y : z) \cdot (u : v : w) = xu : yv : zw`.

.. container:: note-item

   A construction for \* is easily obtained from the construction for "barycentric multiplication" mentioned in connection with :ref:`X_2 <X_2>`, just below.

.. container:: note-item

   The incenter and the other classical centers are discussed in these highly recommended books:

   **Nathan Altshiller Court,** *College Geometry,* Barnes & Noble, 1952;

      * **Roger A. Johnson,** *Advanced Euclidean Geometry,* Dover, 1960.

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``O_A`` be the circle tangent to side :g:`BC <[ B C ]>` at its midpoint and to the circumcircle on the side of :g:`BC <[ B C ]>` opposite A. Define ``O_B`` and ``O_C`` cyclically. Let ``L_A`` be the external tangent to circles ``O_B`` and ``O_C`` that is nearest to ``O_A``. Define ``L_B`` and ``L_C`` cyclically. Let ``A'`` = ``L_B`` ∩\ ``L_C``, and define ``B'`` and ``C'`` cyclically. Then triangle ``A'B'C'`` is homothetic to the :term:`medial triangle <medial-triangle>`, and the center of homothety is :ref:`X_1 <X_1>`; see the reference at :ref:`X_1001 <X_1001>`.

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``A'B'C'`` and ``A″B″C″`` be the intouch and :term:`excentral triangles <excentral-triangle>`; :ref:`X_1 <X_1>` is the radical center of the circumcircles of ``AA'A″``, ``BB'B″``, ``CC'C″``. (:person:`Randy Hutson <randy-hutson>`, December 10, 2016)

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``Eu_aEu_bEu_c`` be the :term:`Feuerbach triangle <feuerbach-triangle>`. Let ``A″`` be the :term:`trilinear pole <trilinear-pole>` of line ``Eu_bEu_c``, and define ``B″`` and ``C″`` cyclically. The lines ``AA″``, ``BB″``, ``CC″`` concur in :ref:`X_1 <X_1>`. (Randy Hutson, November 17, 2019)

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``A'B'C'`` be the mixtilinear excentral triangle. Let ``A″`` be the :term:`cevapoint <cevapoint>` of ``B'`` and ``C'``, and define ``B″`` and ``C″`` cyclically. The lines ``AA″``, ``BB″``, ``CC″`` concur in :ref:`X_1 <X_1>`. (Randy Hutson, November 17, 2019)

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``A'B'C'`` be the mixtilinear excentral triangle. Let ``A″`` be the trilinear pole of line ``B'C'``, and define ``B″`` and ``C″`` cyclically. The lines ``AA″``, ``BB″``, ``CC″`` concur in :ref:`X_1 <X_1>`. (Randy Hutson, November 17, 2019)

.. container:: note-item

   Let ``A'B'C'`` be the mixtilinear excentral triangle. Let ``L_A`` be the :term:`trilinear polar <trilinear-polar>` of ``A'``, and define ``L_B`` and ``L_C`` cyclically. Let ``A″`` = ``L_B``\ ∩\ ``L_C``, and define ``B″`` and ``C″`` cyclically. The lines ``AA″``, ``BB″``, ``CC″`` concur in :ref:`X_1 <X_1>`. (Randy Hutson, November 17, 2019)
