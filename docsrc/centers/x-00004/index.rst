   Let :g:`P <P>` be a point in the plane of :g:`ABC << A B C >>`. Let :g:`O_{a} <O_a>` be the circumcenter of :g:`BCP << B C P >>`, and define :g:`O_{b} <O_b>` and :g:`O_{c} <O_c>` cyclically. Let :g:`Q <Q>` be the circumcenter of :g:`O_{a}O_{b}O_{c} << O_a O_b O_c >>`. :g:`P <P>` = Q only when :g:`P <P>` = :ref:`X_4 <X_4>`. (Randy Hutson, June 27, 2018)

.. container:: note-item

   Taking a reference triangle :g:`ABC << A B C >>` and a variable point :g:`P <P>` on the plane, :g:`P <P>`\ =\ :ref:`X_3 <X_3>` is the point of maximal area of its pedal triangle when considering all points P inside the circumcircle of :g:`ABC << A B C >>`. There are points P far away from the circumcircle for which the area of their pedal triangles is much larger. However, if you consider the signed area of the pedal triangle of P (of which sign depends on whether the points are in clockwise or anti-clockwise order), you could just say that the area of the pedal triangle of :g:`P <P>` is always negative whenever P is outside of the circumcircle so that :g:`P <P>`\ =\ :ref:`X_3 <X_3>` gives the global maximum. (Mark Helman, July 10, 2020)

.. container:: note-item

   A slightly similar thing happens regarding the area of the antipedal triangle of :g:`P <P>`. :g:`P <P>`\ =\ :ref:`X_4 <X_4>` has the smallest area of its antipedal amongst all P in the interior of triangle :g:`ABC << A B C >>` (when :ref:`X_4 <X_4>` is in this interior). There are points P (on the circumcircle) for which this area goes to 0. However, if we consider the signed area of the antipedal, even though there are still regions of the plane outside of :g:`ABC << A B C >>` where the signed area is positive, :g:`P <P>`\ =\ :ref:`X_4 <X_4>` gives the smallest area of the antipedal among all P for which this area is positive (this works even when :g:`ABC << A B C >>` is obtuse, and points close to the circumcircle (on both sides) have negative antipedal area). (Mark Helman, July 10, 2020)
