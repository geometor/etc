.. admonition:: Group Preamble — RS Points and SR Points (Rigby-Simson points and Simson-Rigby points)
   :class: note

   Centers :ref:`X_2677 <X_2677>` to :ref:`X_2770 <X_2770>` are examples of Rigby-Simson points and Simson-Rigby points. These two types of points are discussed in

   Ross Honsberger , Episodes in Nineteenth and Twentieth Century Euclidean Geometry , Mathematical Association of America, 1995. Chapter 11: The Orthopole, pages 124-136.

   Specifically, on pages 132-136, Honsberger defines the "Rigby Point," in honor of John Rigby, as the orthopole of the six sides of two triangles, all of whose vertices lie on a circle, and he notes that this orthopole lies on the six Simson lines of the vertices.

   Now suppose that :g:`P <P>` =\ :math:`p : q : r` (trilinears) and :g:`U <U>` =\ :math:`u : v : w` are distinct points on the circumcircle of a triangle :g:`ABC << A B C >>`. Their Simson lines meet in a point :g:`X <X>` which is here called the Rigby-Simson point of :g:`P <P>` and U, denoted by RS(P,U). That is, RS(P,U) is the orthopole of line :g:`PU <[ P U ]>` with respect to triangle :g:`ABC << A B C >>`.

   On the circumcircle, there is a unique point K whose Simson line is perpendicular to line :g:`PU <[ P U ]>`, and whose Simson line also passes through RS(P,U). The point :g:`K <K>` is here called the Simson-Rigby point of :g:`P <P>` and U, denoted by SR(P,U). Note that SR(P,U) = Λ(P,U)

   If you have The Geometer's Sketchpad, you can view **RS(P,U) and SR(P,U)** (``RigbySimson.gsp``).

   Continuing with contributions of :person:`Peter Moses <peter-moses>` during October, 2004, trilinears for RS(P,U) are

   :math:`f(a,b,c,p,q,r,u,v,w) : f(b,c,a,q,r,p,v,w,u) : f(c,a,b,r,p,q,w,u,v)`, where

   :math:`f(a,b,c,p,q,r,u,v,w) = 1/[a^{2}r^{2}v^{2}(q \cos A - p \cos B)(w \cos A - u \cos C)] - 1/[a^{2}q^{2}w^{2}(r \cos A - p \cos C)(v \cos A - u \cos B)]`,

   and trilinears for SR(P,U), reckoned as the point of intersection of lines P-to-:math:`U ^{- 1}` and U-to-:math:`P ^{- 1}`, are

   (rv - qw)/(qv - rw) : (pw - ru)/(rw - pu) : (qu - pv)/(pu - qv).
