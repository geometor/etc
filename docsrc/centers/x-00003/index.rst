Notes
-----

.. container:: note-item

   As a point on the Euler line, :ref:`X(3) <X(3)>` has Shinagawa coefficients (1, -1).

.. container:: note-item

   :ref:`X(3) <X(3)>` is the point of concurrence of the perpendicular bisectors of the sides of ABC. The lengths of segments AX, BX, CX are equal if and only if X = :ref:`X(3) <X(3)>`. This common distance is the radius of the circumcircle, which passes through vertices A,B,C. Called the circumradius, it is given by R = a/(2 sin A) = abc/(4\*area(ABC)).

.. container:: note-item

   The tangents at vertices of :term:`excentral triangle <excentral-triangle>` to the McCay cubic K003 concur in :ref:`X(3) <X(3)>`. Also, the tangents at A,B,C to the orthocubic K006 concur in :ref:`X(3) <X(3)>`. (:person:`Randy Hutson <randy-hutson>`, November 18, 2015)

.. container:: note-item

   Let A'B'C' be the :term:`cevian triangle <cevian-triangle>` of :ref:`X(4) <X(4)>`. Let A″ be :ref:`X(4) <X(4)>`-of-AB'C', and define B″, C″ cyclically. The lines A″, BB″, CC″ concur in :ref:`X(3) <X(3)>`. (Randy Hutson, June 27, 2018)

.. container:: note-item

   Let P be a point in the plane of ABC. Let P' be the :term:`isogonal conjugate <isogonal-conjugate>` of P. Let P" be the pedal antipodal perspector of P. :ref:`X(3) <X(3)>` is the unique point P for which P' = P". (Randy Hutson, June 27, 2018)

.. container:: note-item

   Taking a reference triangle ABC and a variable point P on the plane, P=\ :ref:`X(3) <X(3)>` is the point of maximal area of its :term:`pedal triangle <pedal-triangle>` when considering all points P inside the circumcircle of ABC. There are points P far away from the circumcircle for which the area of their pedal triangles is much larger. However, if you consider the signed area of the pedal triangle of P (of which sign depends on whether the points are in clockwise or anti-clockwise order), you could just say that the area of the pedal triangle of P is always negative whenever P is outside of the circumcircle so that P=\ :ref:`X(3) <X(3)>` gives the global maximum. (Mark Helman, July 10, 2020)

.. container:: note-item

   A slightly similar thing happens regarding the area of the :term:`antipedal triangle <antipedal-triangle>` of P. P=\ :ref:`X(4) <X(4)>` has the smallest area of its antipedal amongst all P in the interior of triangle ABC (when :ref:`X(4) <X(4)>` is in this interior). There are points P (on the circumcircle) for which this area goes to 0. However, if we consider the signed area of the antipedal, even though there are still regions of the plane outside of ABC where the signed area is positive, P=\ :ref:`X(4) <X(4)>` gives the smallest area of the antipedal among all P for which this area is positive (this works even when ABC is obtuse, and points close to the circumcircle (on both sides) have negative antipedal area). (Mark Helman, July 10, 2020)

.. container:: note-item

   View `Extremal Area Pedal and Antipedal Triangles <https://dan-reznik.github.io/extremal-pedal-antipedal/>`__, by Mark Helman, Ronaldo Garcia, and :person:`Dan Reznik <dan-reznik>`.

.. container:: note-item

   Let T be any one of these trianges: {Aries, :ref:`X(3) <X(3)>`-Ehrmann, X3-ABC reflections, 3rd pedal of :ref:`X(3) <X(3)>`, 3rd antipedal of :ref:`X(3) <X(3)>`, inner-Le Viet An, outer-Le Viet An}. Let O\ :sub:`A` be the circle centered at the A-vertex of T and passing through A; define O\ :sub:`B`\ and O\ :sub:`C` cyclically. :ref:`X(3) <X(3)>` is the radical center of O\ :sub:`A`, O\ :sub:`B`, O\ :sub:`C`. (Randy Hutson, August 30, 2020)

.. container:: note-item

   In the plane of a triangle ABC, let

      * Oa = circle with diameter BC, and define Ob and Oc cyclically;
      * A′ = reflection of A in Oa, and define B′ and C′ cyclically;
      * Ab = BA'∩Oa, and define Bc and Ca cyclically;
      * Ac = CA'∩Oa, and define Ba and Cb cyclically;
      * A1 = BcBa∩CaCb, and define B1 and C1 cyclically.

   The triangle A1B1C1 is perspective to ABC, and the perspector is :ref:`X(3) <X(3)>`. (:person:`Dasari Naga Vijay Krishna <dasari-naga-vijay-krishna>`, April 19, 2021)

.. container:: note-item

   Let O be a point (not necessarily :ref:`X(3) <X(3)>`), and let be a AOB be a fixed-angle sector of a circle C=(O,R), rigidly rotating about center O. Let P be an arbitrary point. One of its foci is O. `Figure (ellipse) <https://user-images.githubusercontent.com/16231062/145676689-1e52d8af-272b-403c-9f6f-8e9de14e192e.png>`__. `Figure (hyperbola) <https://user-images.githubusercontent.com/16231062/145676790-e5b5e328-5498-4a52-94d0-61769a695355.png>`__. .. container:: note-item

   Let A'B'C' be the :term:`anticevian triangle <anticevian-triangle>` of :ref:`X(3) <X(3)>`, and let Ea be the ellipse passing through A' and having foci B' and C'. Define Eb and Ec cyclically.
