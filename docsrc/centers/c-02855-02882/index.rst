.. admonition:: Group Preamble — SM Points (Simson-Moses points)
   :class: note

   Centers :ref:`X_2855 <X_2855>` to :ref:`X_2868 <X_2868>` are examples of Simson-Moses points, defined by changing "isogonal" to "isotomic" in the definition of Simson-Ribgy points (:ref:`X_2687 <X_2687>` to :ref:`X_2770 <X_2770>`. That is, for points :g:`P <P>` and U on the circumcircle of triangle :g:`ABC << A B C >>`, the point of intersection of the lines (P and :term:`isotomic conjugate <isotomic-conjugate>` of :g:`U <U>`) and (U and isotomic conjugate of :g:`P <P>`) defines the Simson-Moses point of :g:`P <P>` and U, denoted by SM(P,U). If P and U are given in barycentric coordinates :math:`p : q : r` and :math:`u : v : w`, then

   (rv - qw)/(qv - rw) : (pw - ru)/(rw - pu) : (qu - pv)/(pu - qv) (barycentrics)

   If P and U are given in trilinear coordinates :math:`p : q : r` and :math:`u : v : w`, then

   :math:`b^{2}`\ :math:`c^{2}(rv - qw)`/(:math:`b^{2}`\ qv - :math:`c^{2}`\ rw) : :math:`c^{2}`\ :math:`a^{2}(pw - ru)`/(:math:`c^{2}`\ rw - :math:`a^{2}`\ pu) : :math:`a^{2}`\ :math:`b^{2}(qu - pv)`/(:math:`a^{2}`\ pu - :math:`b^{2}`\ qv) (trilinears)

   On Oct. 23, 2004, :person:`Peter Moses <peter-moses>` described the point for which the designation SM(P,U) is here introduced, and he noted that SM(P,U), like SR(P,U), lies on the circumcircle.

   In the following list, **the appearance of I, J, K means that RS(X(i),X(j)) = X(K):**

   The definitions of SR and SM points depend on two kinds of :term:`isoconjugates <isoconjugate>` (i.e., isogonal and isotomic). To generalize, suppose

   :g:`X <X>` =\ :math:`x : y : z`, :g:`P <P>` =\ :math:`p : q : r`, :g:`U <U>` =\ :math:`u : v : w`

   are points, none on a sideline of triangle :g:`ABC << A B C >>`. Then

   (X-isoconjugate of :g:`P <P>`) =\ :math:`yzqr : zxrp : xypq` and (X-isoconjugate of :g:`U <U>`) =\ :math:`yzvw : zxwu : xyuv`,

   Denote these points by P(X) and U(X), respectively. It is easy to show using determinants that PU(X) and UP(X) meet in the point. That point defines the *X-Simson point of :g:`P <P>` and :g:`U* <U*>`, denoted by S(X;P,U):

   S(X;P,U) = yz(qw - rv)/(qvy - rwz) : zx(ru - pw)/(rwz - pux) : xy(pv - qu)/(pux - qvy).

   **Theorem: If P and U are on the circumcircle, then S(X;P,U) is on the circumcircle.** A "computer proof" is easy: solve the equations a/p + b/q + c/r = 0 and a/u + b/v + c/w = 0 for r and w, substitute for these in a/r + b/s + c/t, where r, s, t are trilinears for S(X;P,U), and find that a/r + b/s + c/t = 0.

   To conclude, SR(P,U) = S(:ref:`X_1 <X_1>`;P,U) and SM(P,U) = S(:ref:`X_2 <X_2>`;P,U).
